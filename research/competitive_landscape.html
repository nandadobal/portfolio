Beyond the Body Scanner:
A JTBD-Led Competitive Landscape

Neko's real competitive set isn't Prenuvo or Ezra. It's every product that serves the same jobs to be done across the preventive health journey — from skin monitoring apps and CGM trackers to health anxiety tools and longevity clinics.

The core reframe: Most competitive analyses of Neko compare body scan clinics. That's the wrong lens. The better question is: what jobs are Neko's members trying to get done? Those jobs are served by dozens of categories that don't compete on clinic visits at all — apps, wearables, test kits, therapy platforms, and longevity clinics. Neko's real moat isn't against Prenuvo. It's whether it can own the full job across the entire health journey.

Deep Dive · JTBD 03

Skin & Mole Tracking

Neko maps moles during every scan but isn't primarily positioned as a skin health product. The £450M+ AI dermatology market is growing at 27.6% CAGR to 2033. This is a JTBD Neko could own but currently shares with apps that serve it daily — not annually.

The structural gap: Neko scans moles once a year. Moles that matter can change in weeks. SkinVision, Miiskin and others fill the 11-month gap — and build daily habit and brand loyalty doing so. Neko could own this JTBD end-to-end with a between-scan skin monitoring feature.

Market size

AI Dermatology Apps market: $450M in 2025, projected to reach $3.2B by 2033 (27.6% CAGR). Skin cancer is the most common cancer globally — melanoma detection is the #1 use case driving demand.

Key regulatory signal

SkinVision holds CE marking as a regulated medical device in Europe. Skin Analytics has active NHS partnerships for clinical triage. This space is moving toward clinical-grade validation faster than most consumer health verticals.

What Neko does well

  • Full-body 3D mole mapping — no camera error, consistent angle
  • Longitudinal pixel-by-pixel comparison year-on-year
  • Doctor in the loop for any concerning finding
  • Non-radiological — safe for annual full-body coverage

What Neko doesn't do

  • No between-scan monitoring (11-month gap)
  • No push alert if a mole the member photographs at home looks concerning
  • No integration with apps like Miiskin for ongoing tracking
  • No dermatologist referral pathway built into the product

Opportunity for the interview

A skin monitoring feature in the Neko app — using phone camera with AI guided by the Neko scan baseline — closes the annual gap, adds a daily engagement touchpoint, and creates a genuine moat. The Neko baseline scan creates a personalised dermoscopic map that no app can replicate without proprietary hardware.

Partnership vs build

Skin Analytics has NHS partnerships and clinical validation. A Neko × Skin Analytics integration — where Neko's mole map feeds into Skin Analytics' between-scan triage — could be faster and more clinically defensible than building in-house.

Deep Dive · JTBD 08

Health Anxiety

Health anxiety (illness anxiety disorder) affects an estimated 4–8% of the population — but overrepresents in preventive health consumers. The question of whether body scanning helps or worsens anxiety is unresolved and clinically important. This is the hardest design problem in Neko's product.

The double-edged sword: People with health anxiety are among Neko's most motivated customers. But they are also the segment most at risk of a poorly-designed results experience causing harm — spiralling into obsessive re-scanning, false-positive panic, or distorted self-perception. Prenuvo explicitly acknowledges this on their blog. Neko doesn't appear to.

What the evidence says

The literature on scanning and health anxiety is mixed. CBT and ERP (Exposure and Response Prevention) have strong evidence for reducing health anxiety — over 70% improvement rate. Whether preventive scanning calms or amplifies anxiety depends almost entirely on how results are communicated and what support exists after.

Who serves this JTBD today

  • NoCD — OCD/health anxiety treatment via ERP
  • Woebot — AI CBT chatbot; proven for anxiety reduction
  • BetterHelp / Calm — therapy access and mindfulness
  • Headspace Health — AI Emotional Resilience module (Apr 2025)
  • GP reassurance — but access is scarce and time-limited

Design implications for Neko

  • Result framing matters more than result content — the same data can reassure or terrify depending on language and context
  • The doctor consultation must be designed for emotional containment, not just information delivery
  • The AI summary layer needs to know when to de-escalate — and when to escalate to a human
  • Repeat scanning cadence should be driven by clinical need, not anxiety-driven demand

Opportunity for the interview

Neko's AI/LLM layer is the first line of emotional containment after a scan. The member summary should be designed with progressive disclosure — start with the overall message ("your cardiovascular health looks good"), then invite deeper exploration. Never lead with a long list of flags. The model should have an explicit anxiety de-escalation persona, not just a clinical readout mode.

Synthesis

Product Gaps & Opportunities

Based on competitive research, user reviews, and JTBD mapping — where does Neko leave demand unserved? These are ordered by strategic priority for the AI/LLM role.

1. Between-Scan Engagement Desert
High Priority

JTBD: Data interpretation, chronic tracking, behaviour change

The member app is passive — results live there but nothing happens for 11 months. Users pay £299 once a year and then feel abandoned. The 80% rebooking rate suggests loyalty is real, but it could be much higher with ongoing AI engagement. Competitors like ZOE, Levels, Oura, and Thriva win on between-visit stickiness.

Opportunity: An AI health companion that interprets the scan results over time — suggests lifestyle changes, answers questions at 2am, tracks progress on flagged biomarkers, and connects the annual scan to daily decisions. This is exactly what the Principal PM - AI/LLM role should own.

2. Post-Scan Specialist Navigation
High Priority

JTBD: Early detection follow-through, healthcare navigation

14.1% of Neko members require further medical treatment — but the product ends at the doctor consultation. Members are left to self-navigate finding a cardiologist, dermatologist, or endocrinologist. User reviews flag this as a real pain point: "the scan was great but I had no idea what to do next."

Opportunity: AI-powered care navigation — the Neko app helps members act on their results by finding, booking, and briefing the right specialist. Neko's doctor-written brief becomes the referral letter. This closes the most critical gap in the clinical journey.

3. Skin Monitoring Between Scans
High Priority

JTBD: Skin & mole monitoring (JTBD 03)

Neko maps every mole at year-one. But users are on their own for the 11 months between scans. SkinVision and Miiskin fill this gap — and are building loyalty and habit with it. Neko's mole map is the best possible baseline for a between-scan monitoring feature.

Opportunity: A guided photo tool in the Neko app — "check this mole monthly" — where the AI compares member photos against the Neko mole map baseline. Only Neko has the baseline to make this genuinely accurate. Partnership with Skin Analytics or SkinVision is a faster path than building the AI from scratch.

4. Health Anxiety Experience Design
Medium Priority

JTBD: Health reassurance & anxiety management (JTBD 08)

User reviews mention anxiety-amplifying experiences: false positives, clinical language in results, inconsistent doctor consultations. The segment most likely to be on Neko's waitlist (health-aware, affluent, anxious about their future) is also the most likely to be harmed by a poorly-designed results moment.

Opportunity: A results experience explicitly designed for progressive disclosure and emotional safety — starting with a clear "here's the headline" summary, then layering detail. The AI/LLM layer needs distinct communication modes: reassurance-first for clean results; careful, compassionate escalation for findings.

5. Wearable Data Integration
Medium Priority

JTBD: Wearable data interpretation (JTBD 09)

32% of consumers now use AI for health information. Oura, Apple Watch, Whoop users generate daily streams of HRV, sleep, and heart data — but have no clinical context to interpret it. Neko has the clinical baseline that makes wearable data meaningful.

Opportunity: Neko as the "clinical anchor" for wearable data — a member's Oura readiness score means something different when you know their resting heart rate baseline, VO2max, and cardiovascular risk profile from a Neko scan. Integration with Apple Health / Oura API is a clear product bet.

6. Family History & Risk Context
Lower Priority

JTBD: Early detection (JTBD 02)

Family history is the strongest predictor of risk for most conditions Neko detects — but it's collected via a pre-scan questionnaire and then disappears. The AI layer could use it to personalise interpretation: "your blood pressure is 128/82 — that's borderline normal, but given your family history of hypertension, here's what I'd recommend."

Opportunity: Family risk profiling baked into the AI interpretation layer — turning static questionnaire data into dynamic personalised context for every result narrative. A relatively low-complexity product bet with high differentiation value.

Interview Edge

What's Missing from Standard Neko Research

Things that won't appear in the average competitor analysis — but that would signal genuine depth to the Neko interview team.

The Digital Twin is already real

Neko already does pixel-by-pixel volumetric comparison between annual scans — not just data comparison, but a literal 3D model delta. This is infrastructure that no app competitor can build. It's underemphasised in public comms and should be front-and-centre in the AI product narrative.

Angle: "The data moat isn't the scan — it's the longitudinal delta model. The AI layer has to be designed around that."

AI health chatbots are becoming commoditized fast

Amazon Health AI, Google, Apple, and Microsoft all launched consumer health AI in early 2026. 32% of consumers already use AI chatbots for health (double from a year ago). Neko's AI layer cannot compete on the interface — it has to win on the proprietary clinical data underneath it.

Angle: "A generic LLM can translate lab results. Only Neko's LLM knows your full-body scan baseline."

Neko's 2025 data story: 6x scan growth

Neko delivered 6x more scans in 2025 vs 2024. Global signups exceed 300,000. Their first longitudinal cohort (1,469 members) showed measurable improvements in blood pressure, cholesterol, and blood sugar from scan 1 to scan 2. This is the product working — and is the foundation of a very compelling outcomes narrative.

Angle: "The data flywheel is accelerating faster than the product layer. The AI/LLM role is how Neko closes that gap."

GRAIL Galleri as a structural threat

GRAIL's Galleri blood test detects 50+ cancer types from a single blood draw — and has active NHS trial partnerships. If liquid biopsy becomes mainstream for cancer detection, it reduces one of Neko's core value propositions. Neko's response must be: scanning catches what liquid biopsy misses (cardiovascular structure, skin, metabolic, MSK).

Angle: Shows you understand the adjacent science, not just the direct competitive set.

Numan: the UK competitor nobody mentions

Numan — UK-native, D2C, launched an AI health assistant in 2025 — offers blood tests, GP consultations, and AI-personalised health guidance. Not a scanning company, but serves JTBD 01, 04, and 10 in the same UK market and demographic. Worth a pointed reference as the "closest AI health companion" competitor.

Angle: "The real UK race is between clinic-led models like Neko and lab + AI companion models like Numan."

The health anxiety design problem has no solved answer

No competitor has elegantly solved how to communicate health risk data to a health-anxious consumer. Prenuvo acknowledges it on their blog; Neko doesn't appear to have a public position. This is both a product gap and a genuine interview differentiator — showing you've thought about the emotional design of the results experience.

Angle: "The hardest design challenge isn't the AI — it's knowing when not to tell people everything at once."

Between-scan is where loyalty is actually built

80% rebooking sounds great — but ZOE has daily engagement. Oura has daily engagement. Thriva has quarterly touchpoints. Neko has one visit a year. The members who stay loyal do so despite the engagement desert, not because of it. The AI companion layer has an enormous opportunity to make Neko feel like a year-round relationship.

Angle: "The scan is the acquisition moment. The AI layer is the retention engine."

NYC launch spring 2026 — timing is everything

Neko is opening in NYC in spring 2026. The US market has no equivalent (Prenuvo is MRI-based and radiological; Function Health is lab-only). The Principal PM role will ship into this expansion. Any product feature discussion should be framed with US launch timing, FDA regulatory constraints, and the different US consumer context in mind.

Angle: "What I'd ship in the first 90 days of the NYC launch to differentiate from day one."
