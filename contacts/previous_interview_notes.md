## Useful Framing from That Process

**On product/feature launch:** The Cleo re-architecture story — brought leadership on the journey with a POC, clear four-metric framework, rollout to 100% users.

**On most impactful product:** Batelle AI story — phased risk approach (feasibility → viability → usability), messaging going from 80% human to 16% human.

**On prioritisation:** Quarterly OKRs, RICE + data, bi-weekly sprints, MoSCoW. PLG example with funnel learning (only 20% viewing personalised plan, 2x more likely to convert within 24h).

**On favourite product:** Peloton — weekly streaks over daily, social without interaction, but suffering from feature bloat. Would simplify home screen categories and persist user filters.

**On staying user-focused:** In-person visits (menopause cafes, schools, users' homes). JTBD / Demand-Side Sales (Bob Moesta). 10/10 pain point scale. Making research accessible to engineers.

**On failure:** Bia Care 12-week programme — high NPS, strong waitlist, but no willingness to pay. Learnings: test WTP early; what people say they want ≠ what they pay for.

**On stakeholder management:** Batelle newborn product — design sprint, aligned with ops and growth leads, 10% of sales now mostly organic. Disagreed and committed (affordable product overruled by founder; came back to it later and proved the case).

**On cross-functional teams:** Ops board at Batelle. Innovator track that evolved into PM pipeline. Two-pronged AI adoption: education + ground-up advocates.

## Questions Worth Asking in Interviews

- What does success look like in months 1–3?
- How are decisions made — founder-led, data-led?
- Who is successful here? What kind of competent person isn't?
- How do product and engineering work together? Is PM owner or facilitator?
- What are the biggest goals/opportunities/risks for the business?
- KPIs across acquisition and retention — why those metrics, how defined?
