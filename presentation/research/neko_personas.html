Market Context & Framing

Neko Health's user base isn't simply "health-conscious people." The motivations are highly specific — and critically, they differ between the UK and US in ways that affect product design, messaging, and where an AI/LLM layer can add most value.

In the UK, Neko fills a structural gap: the NHS delivers reactive, not preventive, care. Neko's users are often expressing frustration with a system they believe in but feel let down by. The emotional register is quieter — reassurance-seeking, not optimization-obsessing.

In the US (particularly New York), the context flips. Health optimisation is a cultural identity marker among Neko's likely early adopters. Function Health, Levels, and the Attia/Huberman ecosystem have primed these users to think in biomarkers and protocols. They're not seeking reassurance; they're seeking an imaging layer for their stack. Willingness to pay is higher, but so is the expectation of data sophistication.

🇬🇧 UK Context

  • NHS = free reactive care, essentially zero preventive offering
  • GP wait times avg. 2–3 weeks; specialist referrals can take months
  • Private health checks (Bupa MOT, Nuffield) exist but feel corporate, not personal
  • Competitors: Thriva (blood), ZOE (nutrition/CGM), BUPA/Nuffield health checks
  • £299/yr is meaningful spend; justified by "filling the NHS gap" narrative
  • Skin care / dermatology wait times: 3–6 months on NHS

🇺🇸 US (NYC) Context

  • Insurance system: preventive coverage varies wildly; many consumers frustrated
  • Longevity/biohacking culture is mainstream among affluent early adopters
  • Function Health ($499/yr), Parsley Health ($375/mo), HLI ($25k+) set expectations
  • NYC: time-poor executives, concierge medicine familiarity, HSA/FSA spending
  • Peter Attia, Huberman, Rhonda Patrick have educated consumers on biomarkers
  • Higher price tolerance; "comprehensive" scan expected to include deep data
🇬🇧 United Kingdom — Existing Market

UK Persona Profiles

Users shaped by NHS dependency, private-sector frustration, and a growing but culturally reserved interest in personal health data.

UK Persona 01

The NHS-Frustrated Professional

Ages 35–50 · Urban professional · £65k–£130k household income
JTBD 01 · Reassurance JTBD 10 · NHS Gap-Fill
"I trust the NHS, I really do. But I'm 44 and I've never had a comprehensive check. My GP appointment took three weeks and lasted eight minutes. I'm not ill — I just want to know I'm not about to become ill."

Key Motivations

  • Time-poor; can't afford to take sick days or wait on long referral chains
  • Mild anxiety about being "fine now but silently not fine" — no baseline data
  • Has tried to get a 'health MOT' via NHS; was told there's no such thing unless symptomatic
  • Professional identity tied to being capable and on top of things — health is a blind spot

What They Reach For Today

  • Occasional private GP appointment for a specific concern
  • Nuffield / Bupa Executive Health Check (feels impersonal, expensive, one-off)
  • Thriva home blood test kits (useful but limited; no imaging)

What Neko Offers Them

A complete, non-radiological body scan in under 30 minutes — systemic, not symptomatic. It's the check-up the NHS doesn't provide and Bupa makes feel like a corporate benefit rather than personal care. The annual cadence fits their rhythm; the clean interface matches their professional standards.

⚠ Watch Out

If results surface an unexpected finding, this persona may not have a clear next step — they're in the NHS system and returning to it for follow-up is the same maze they were frustrated by. Neko needs a credible "what happens next" pathway or this persona's satisfaction turns to anxiety post-scan.

UK Persona 02

The Family History Carrier

Ages 40–55 · Parent or sibling with cancer or heart disease
JTBD 02 · Early Detection
"My mum was diagnosed with bowel cancer at 51. She said she felt completely fine right up until she didn't. I think about that constantly. I don't want to wait until I'm symptomatic."

Key Motivations

  • Visceral, personal motivation — a parent or sibling's diagnosis changed their calculus
  • Urgency is real and emotionally grounded, not anxiety-driven speculation
  • Wants to be a proactive patient, not a passive one; feels duty to family to stay well
  • NHS cancer screening programs start at 50–60; they feel unprotected in the gap

What They Reach For Today

  • Lobbying GP for earlier colonoscopy / mammography referral (often refused)
  • Genetic testing (Myriad, 23andMe health report) — BRCA, Lynch syndrome
  • Private oncology consultations; feels expensive and reactive rather than preventive

What Neko Offers Them

Annual whole-body scanning that catches morphological changes before symptoms appear. For this persona, Neko isn't a wellness luxury — it's a genuine early detection tool. The value proposition is simple and emotionally resonant: catch it early, when it's still fixable.

⚠ Watch Out

This is Neko's most clinically motivated user — they will scrutinise the scan's sensitivity and specificity for their specific cancer type. If Neko's scan doesn't image the organ they're most worried about (e.g., bowel), or if the AI-assisted interpretation glosses over their family-specific risk, they'll feel underserved. Transparency about what the scan does and doesn't detect is non-negotiable here.

UK Persona 03

The Longevity Optimizer

Ages 30–45 · Tech / finance / startup · Wearable power user
JTBD 05 · Longevity JTBD 09 · Wearable Data
"I've got 18 months of HRV data, my sleep score, my VO2 max estimate. But I have literally no idea what's actually happening inside my body. These numbers are proxies. I want the real data."

Key Motivations

  • Already embedded in the quantified self ecosystem: Oura, WHOOP, Apple Watch, Garmin
  • ZOE subscriber; has done a Thriva panel; reads Peter Attia's "Outlive"
  • Wants clinical-grade baseline data to anchor and contextualise wearable outputs
  • Health is an intellectual project as much as an anxiety response — they enjoy the data

What They Reach For Today

  • Regular Thriva / Medichecks blood panels (self-funded)
  • CGM trials (Libre, Dexcom) via ZOE or self-directed
  • Private GP for testosterone, thyroid, extended lipid panels

What Neko Offers Them

The imaging layer that's been missing from their stack. Neko's scan gives them morphological and cardiovascular data that no wearable or blood panel can provide. For this persona, Neko is less a reassurance product and more a data enrichment product — and the AI interpretation layer is what makes it sing. They want to understand trends, not just receive a PDF.

⚠ Watch Out

This is a sophisticated and demanding user. They'll compare Neko's output to Function Health's 100-biomarker panel and ask why they can't download their DICOM files. They want API integrations, trend graphs, and the ability to share data with their private GP. If the product feels closed or the AI explanations feel dumbed down, they'll be vocal critics. The AI/LLM experience is especially important here — it must be substantive, not just reassuring.

UK Persona 04

The Health Anxious

Ages 28–45 · High health awareness; anxiety tipping point varies
JTBD 01 · Reassurance JTBD 08 · Health Anxiety
"I know googling symptoms is a terrible idea and I do it anyway. The doctor says I'm fine but I don't believe her. I thought getting the scan would help but honestly I'm also scared of what it might find."

Key Motivations

  • Genuine desire for reassurance, but with a complicated relationship to the reassurance itself
  • Symptom-googling is compulsive; each clean result provides temporary relief then resets
  • Over-represented in preventive health spending — high motivation to seek products
  • A clean scan result is enormously valuable to them… until a new symptom appears

What They Reach For Today

  • NHS GP appointments "just to check" — often given reassurance but leaves unsatisfied
  • Health anxiety forums and Reddit communities
  • Multiple at-home test kits (stool, blood, urine) with limited interpretive support

What Neko Offers Them

A credible, clinically-grounded baseline that is harder to dismiss than a GP's verbal reassurance. "Your scan shows no abnormalities" lands differently than "you seem fine to me." The annual cadence also provides a natural reset point. The AI layer, if well-designed, can deliver reassurance in a tone that is measured and grounding rather than alarming.

⚠ Watch Out

This is Neko's most ethically complex persona. A scan that returns any ambiguous finding — even a routine "watch and wait" — can significantly worsen health anxiety. The AI/LLM interpretation layer carries enormous responsibility: it must contextualise incidental findings carefully, avoid catastrophising language, and know when to gently redirect to clinical support. Over-servicing this persona's anxiety without a clinical safety net is a product risk as well as a moral one.

UK Persona 05

The Skin-Aware

Ages 25–60 · Had a mole scare, family history of melanoma, or sun-damaged skin
JTBD 03 · Skin Monitoring
"I asked my GP about a mole I was worried about. She said it looked fine but referred me to dermatology 'just to be safe.' That was eight months ago. I'm still waiting."

Key Motivations

  • Specific, concrete concern rather than general health anxiety
  • NHS dermatology wait times are a genuine system failure — 3–6 months for non-urgent
  • Often motivated by a past scare, family history (melanoma), or return from sunny climate
  • Wants ongoing monitoring, not a one-off assessment — skin changes over time

What They Reach For Today

  • At-home skin analysis apps (SkinVision, Miiskin) — useful but non-clinical
  • Private dermatology consultation (£150–£300 per visit; sporadic)
  • Self-monitoring with photography; no baseline or change-detection capability

What Neko Offers Them

Full-body skin scanning with AI-assisted mole analysis as part of a wider health check — not a standalone triage product but an integrated one. The annual cadence enables change detection. For this persona, the skin component may actually be the entry point that brings them to Neko, but the whole-body scan is what retains them.

⚠ Watch Out

Expectations around skin scanning are high — and clinically, dermatology AI has a well-documented bias problem around darker skin tones. If Neko's skin scanning feature doesn't perform equitably across skin types, or if the AI flags something that turns out to be benign after a £250 private dermatology follow-up, trust erodes fast. This persona needs a clear, credible referral pathway if something is flagged.

🇺🇸 United States — NYC Launch Market

US Persona Profiles

Early adopters shaped by the longevity culture, insurance frustration, biohacking identity, and significantly higher willingness to pay for premium health experiences.

US Persona 01

The Proactive Executive

Ages 40–55 · NYC/SF/LA · $200k–$500k+ HHI · C-suite or partner track
JTBD 01 · Reassurance JTBD 05 · Longevity
"I optimise everything in my life. My diet, my sleep, my workouts, my calendar. But I realised I had no idea what was actually happening inside my body. That felt like an obvious gap to fix."

Key Motivations

  • Control orientation — health is just another domain to optimise systematically
  • Time is their scarcest resource; sub-30-minute scan is a genuine differentiator
  • Already pays for concierge medicine ($3k–$10k/yr) but it's reactive, not imaging-based
  • Health as executive fitness — being on top of this signals competence and longevity

What They Reach For Today

  • Concierge primary care (One Medical, Atria Health, MDVIP) for same-day access
  • Executive physicals at Cleveland Clinic or Johns Hopkins (~$3,000–$5,000)
  • Full-body MRI scans via Prenuvo ($2,500 one-off) — Neko's direct competitor

What Neko Offers Them

A compelling price-to-comprehensiveness ratio: $400–500/year vs $2,500 for a Prenuvo MRI, with a faster turnaround and a technology-forward experience that aligns with their identity. The annual membership model fits their preference for subscriptions over one-off transactions. Neko's Spotify provenance also resonates as a marker of product sophistication.

⚠ Watch Out

This persona is accustomed to white-glove service. If Neko's NYC clinic feels clinical rather than premium, or if the digital experience feels behind their concierge medicine app, they'll leave. They're also likely to push for full MRI capability — Neko's non-radiological approach needs a confident, articulate answer for why that's sufficient or even advantageous. Customer success matters as much as the product itself.

US Persona 02

The Longevity-Optimised Biohacker

Ages 30–45 · Tech or finance · Podcast listener · Stack-builder
JTBD 05 · Longevity JTBD 09 · Wearable Data
"I'm a Function Health member, I use Levels CGM, I've done a DEXA scan, I do zone 2 four times a week. I read Outlive twice. I want Neko to be my imaging layer. What does my scan actually look like?"

Key Motivations

  • Has a sophisticated mental model of health biomarkers — ApoB, Lp(a), CAC scoring
  • Already spending $2,000–$5,000/year across health optimisation products
  • Sees Neko as filling the imaging gap that blood panels and wearables can't reach
  • Interested in longitudinal data — how does their arterial health change year on year?

What They Reach For Today

  • Function Health ($499/yr) for 100+ biomarker blood panels
  • Levels or Nutrisense CGM for metabolic health tracking
  • Prenuvo full-body MRI (one-off, $2,500) for imaging baseline

What Neko Offers Them

The annual imaging layer at a fraction of Prenuvo's cost, integrated into their existing stack. The AI interpretation layer must speak their language — CIMT, cardiac output, adipose tissue distribution — not just "everything looks normal." This persona will test the AI's depth immediately. If it holds up, they become Neko's most powerful word-of-mouth advocates (they literally podcast about their health stack).

⚠ Watch Out

Extremely high expectations for data access and interoperability. They will ask: Can I download my DICOM files? Can Neko integrate with Apple Health? Can I share results with my concierge GP? Can I see trends across years? If Neko's data ecosystem is closed, this persona feels locked out of their own health data — a dealbreaker. The AI/LLM UX needs to be genuinely intelligent, not a chatbot wrapper on a PDF.

US Persona 03

The Family History Carrier (US)

Ages 38–55 · Has done 23andMe or Ambry genetics panel · Insurance-aware
JTBD 02 · Early Detection
"My dad had a heart attack at 54. My insurance covers a colonoscopy at 45 — that's it. I asked about a cardiac scan and they said it wasn't medically necessary. I'll pay out of pocket. This is my life."

Key Motivations

  • Motivated by lived experience of watching a family member's health crisis
  • Frustrated by insurance gatekeeping on preventive imaging ("not medically necessary")
  • More likely than UK equivalent to have done genetic testing (23andMe, Invitae)
  • Willing to pay out of pocket specifically because insurance has failed them on this

What They Reach For Today

  • Direct-to-consumer cardiac screenings (Life Line Screening, ~$150)
  • Calcium scoring CT scan ($75–$200 out of pocket — if they know it exists)
  • Genetic counselling and testing to understand inherited risk

What Neko Offers Them

A comprehensive annual scan that doesn't require insurance pre-authorisation or a physician referral. The "just book it" accessibility is a major selling point for someone who has hit walls with their insurer. Neko is the system end-run — the thing they can actually do, now, without waiting for bureaucratic permission. Family history context in the AI interpretation layer would be particularly meaningful here.

⚠ Watch Out

This persona has high expectations about what specifically the scan covers. If their worry is cardiac and Neko's cardiac imaging is less thorough than a dedicated echo or CAC scan, they'll feel misled. The US market also has strong liability awareness — any language that suggests Neko is a diagnostic tool (vs screening/monitoring) creates FDA regulatory exposure and may deter clinicians from engaging with Neko results.

US Persona 04

The Uninsured / Self-Insured Optimizer

Ages 30–50 · Entrepreneur, freelancer, or self-employed · No employer health plan
JTBD 01 · Reassurance JTBD 10 · System Gap-Fill (US)
"I have a catastrophic plan with a $7,000 deductible. Preventive care is basically on me. I think of Neko as an investment — way cheaper than what it would cost to catch something late."

Key Motivations

  • Structurally similar to UK's NHS-frustrated professional but for opposite reasons
  • No employer-sponsored plan; prevention spending comes entirely out of pocket or HSA/FSA
  • ROI framing: Neko as cheap insurance against a catastrophic out-of-pocket treatment cost
  • Entrepreneurial mindset — tends toward direct action rather than bureaucratic navigation

What They Reach For Today

  • Direct primary care (DPC) memberships (~$100/mo) for affordable primary access
  • HSA-funded labs and imaging; motivated to use that money strategically
  • Function Health or Everlywell panels — DTC blood testing without physician gating

What Neko Offers Them

An affordable, comprehensive annual screen that fits the "invest in your health, not in your sickness" mindset. HSA/FSA eligibility would be a significant conversion driver for this persona — make it clear upfront. The membership framing also appeals: they're already in a DPC model, they understand subscription healthcare. Neko is the imaging equivalent of their DPC relationship.

⚠ Watch Out

Pricing sensitivity is real even among higher earners in this segment. If Neko's NYC pricing lands at $600–800 without HSA eligibility clarity, this persona does the math and hesitates. They're also likely to want Neko to replace rather than supplement their care model — a risk if Neko's scope is narrower than they expect. Clear scope communication upfront prevents disappointment.

US Persona 05

The Wearable Power User

Ages 28–45 · Apple Watch / Oura user · Something got flagged
JTBD 09 · Wearable Interpretation JTBD 04 · Data Interpretation
"My Apple Watch has been showing irregular rhythm notifications for three weeks. My doctor said it's probably nothing, ordered an ECG, said it was fine. But it's still doing it. I want a real answer, not another app."

Key Motivations

  • A wearable has surfaced a signal they can't get the medical system to take seriously
  • Frustrated by the gap between consumer-grade sensing and clinical validation
  • Wants a human-in-the-loop interpretation that goes beyond "algorithm says watch this"
  • Not necessarily health-anxious — more technically curious and legitimately concerned

What They Reach For Today

  • Cardiologist referral (wait times 2–4 weeks; often requires PCP gatekeeping)
  • KardiaMobile personal ECG device ($99) for more granular self-monitoring
  • Reddit and health forums trying to contextualise their specific readings

What Neko Offers Them

Clinical-grade cardiovascular and structural imaging that can actually validate or contextualise what the wearable is detecting. The AI/LLM interpretation layer is the centrepiece for this persona — they want a clear, authoritative explanation of what the scan found in relation to their wearable signal. Neko bridges the gap between consumer sensing and clinical certainty better than any other product in this category.

⚠ Watch Out

High expectations for wearable data integration. If Neko can't receive or contextualise their Apple Health or Oura data at intake, the "bring your wearable context" experience falls flat. This persona also needs a clear, confident clinical escalation path — if Neko's scan does confirm something the wearable was flagging, the referral pathway must be immediate and reliable. Being the product that confirms a cardiac finding with no clinical handoff is a serious liability.
