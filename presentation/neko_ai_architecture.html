03 — The Intelligence Layer

Tools vs RAG — why Neko needs both

RAG makes the AI medically literate. Tools make it personally relevant. Together they're what separates a health knowledge base from a health intelligence layer.

≋
RAG
Retrieval Augmented Generation
Before generating a response, search a knowledge base and hand the relevant pieces to the model as context. Like briefing a consultant with a folder of documents before the meeting — they read what's relevant, then answer.
At Neko, RAG handles
Clinical guidelines for cardiovascular risk, metabolic markers, and skin findings
Neko's curated biomarker interpretation guides — what ranges mean for this specific scan type
Medical literature and the evidence base for recommendations
Standard escalation protocols — when a finding requires a doctor vs a watch flag
Static knowledge. The AI learns what medicine knows.
⚡
Tools
Function calling during reasoning
The AI actively calls specific functions as it reasons — deciding what to look up and what actions to take. Like giving the consultant a laptop during the meeting so they can query live data in real time.
At Neko, tools handle
get_member_scan_history(id, biomarker, n)
Retrieve LDL across last 3 scans to detect a trend
calculate_risk_delta(profile, scan)
Compute personalised risk change since last year
check_flagged_findings(member_id)
Look up outstanding doctor follow-ups for this member
route_to_clinical_review(finding, severity)
Escalate a serious finding before it surfaces to the member
Dynamic and action-taking. The AI learns what you need.
◈
Why you need both

RAG alone produces a knowledgeable AI that can talk about health generally — but it has no idea who you are. Tools alone can access your data but don't know what to do with it clinically. Together, the model retrieves clinical context and queries your specific history, producing an insight that is both medically grounded and personally relevant. This is the same architectural shift I led at Cleo — moving from hardcoded topic handling to a tools-based model where the AI could call whatever capability it needed. That's what unlocked the ability to have a conversation about anything.

04 — Triggers & Outcomes

From trigger to insight to impact

The trigger engine decides what's worth surfacing and when. Each trigger generates an insight — and tracking what comes next requires two time horizons: what you can measure now, and what you're actually trying to change.

⊕
Scan Trigger
Fires when: new scan completes
Insight generated
"Your cholesterol improved 11% — here are your top 3 priorities before next year's scan, and one thing your doctor will want to discuss today."
Proximate
Did the member open and read the summary? Did they arrive at their consultation prepared?
Distal
Did biomarkers continue improving at the next scan? Did engagement predict rebooking?
↗
Trend Trigger
Fires when: a pattern persists across 2+ scans
Insight generated
"Your waist circumference has increased two scans in a row. This is now a pattern — here's what typically drives it and what to discuss with your doctor."
Proximate
Did the member acknowledge the trend? Did they adjust lifestyle goals or schedule a GP appointment?
Distal
Did the trend reverse at the next scan? Did early action prevent a metabolic finding?
◷
Temporal Trigger
Fires when: 8 weeks before next scan
Insight generated
"Your scan is in 8 weeks. Based on last year, here are the two areas where focused effort now will move the needle most."
Proximate
Did the member engage with pre-scan guidance? Did they complete the recommended actions before arriving?
Distal
Did the 8-week window produce measurable biomarker improvement at the scan?
◆
Action Trigger
Fires when: doctor flags a finding for follow-up
Insight generated
"4 weeks ago your doctor recommended a follow-up on your ECG finding. Here's what that appointment should cover and how to request it."
Proximate
Did the member book the follow-up? Did they acknowledge the reminder within 7 days?
Distal
Did the member receive appropriate care in time? Was the finding resolved or correctly escalated?
05 — Why this outperforms general AI

The same question. A different answer.

General models know medicine. Neko knows you. The difference isn't the model — it's the depth of personalization the health context object makes possible.

General-purpose AI
"My LDL is 112. What does that mean?"
An LDL of 112 mg/dL is considered near-optimal to borderline high, depending on your cardiovascular risk factors. The American Heart Association recommends keeping LDL below 100 mg/dL for most adults.

Lifestyle changes that may help include reducing saturated fat intake, increasing soluble fiber, and regular aerobic exercise. You should discuss your levels with your doctor if you have other risk factors such as high blood pressure or a family history of heart disease.
Neko AI
"My LDL is 112. What does that mean?"
Your LDL is 112 — down 8% from last year's 122. That improvement lines up with the dietary changes you made after your last scan. Your overall cardiovascular risk profile is now low.

The one thing to watch: your waist circumference has increased two scans in a row, which can affect LDL over time. Your doctor will want to discuss this alongside your ECG finding today — here's how to prepare for that conversation.
06 — Eval Framework

How we know if it's working

Eval quality is the first metric, not the last. If the AI isn't clinically appropriate, nothing else matters — before conversion, before retention.

Offline eval — before launch
Doctor-as-judge
Clinical team rates a sample of AI summaries on accuracy, appropriateness, and escalation correctness. The ground truth proxy.
Blind comparison
Same input to Neko AI and a general model. Do doctors consistently prefer Neko's output? This is the "outperforms" test.
Red-teaming
Adversarial inputs: member reports chest pain at midnight, member says they'll ignore a flag, member asks "do I have cancer?" Does the AI route correctly?
Regression suite
As the model improves, ensure past good outputs don't degrade. Quality gates before every release.
Online eval — post launch
Comprehension rate
Do members actually understand their summary? Comprehension ≠ having read it. Requires a short check.
Doctor conversation quality
Do doctors report members arriving better prepared? Gold standard — measurable and clinically meaningful.
Escalation precision/recall
When AI flags for clinical review, how often was it right? When it didn't flag, was it ever wrong?
Rebooking correlation
Does higher AI engagement predict rebooking? The business case for the intelligence layer.
The hardest problem — closing the loop

You can't fully validate clinical AI in months. If Neko's AI tells a member their metabolic markers suggest pre-diabetic risk, you won't know for 1–2 years whether that was right and whether the actions helped. That's why outcome tracking infrastructure must be built from day one — even when you can't use it yet. We optimize on proximate outcomes in the short term, and build toward validating those proxies against distal outcomes over time. Proximate signals that don't eventually predict distal ones aren't worth optimizing.

07 — Member Journey

The AI's role at every moment

The member experience is a series of emotional states. The AI earns its place at each one — or loses trust if it gets it wrong.

⊕
Immediately after scan
Anxious — "Did they find anything?"
The member has sat in a scanner for an hour and doesn't know what was found. This is the highest emotional volatility moment in the entire experience.
AI role: immediate reassurance and calibration. "Here's what we looked at, here's the overall picture, and here's what your doctor will cover today." No serious findings surfaced here — that's the doctor's role.
◈
That evening, alone
Overwhelmed — "What does all this actually mean?"
Without the AI, the alternative is Googling symptoms at midnight. This is where a general model is most likely to cause harm — and where Neko's personalization matters most.
AI role: translate the health context object into the 3 things that matter, grounded in their specific results. Not population averages — their numbers, their trend, their next action.
◆
Weeks 1–4
Motivated but drifting — "What should I actually do?"
Good intentions fade without clear, specific next steps. The action trigger fires here if the doctor flagged anything. Even without a flag, follow-through needs structure.
AI role: the 3 concrete actions, follow-up on flagged findings at the right cadence — not so frequent it's noise, not so rare it's forgotten.
↗
Months 6–10
Disconnected — "Am I making progress?"
Post-scan motivation fades. The longitudinal value of Neko's data becomes the product here — not a one-time snapshot, but a health trajectory. This is also where Neko differentiates from a one-off check-up.
AI role: surface indicators of progress or regression. Keep the member connected to their health between scans — with wearable integration, lifestyle inputs, or simple check-ins tied to their priorities.
◷
8 weeks before next scan
Re-engaged — "What should I focus on before I go back?"
The temporal trigger fires. The member has a concrete goal: show up to the next scan with better numbers. This is the moment highest-intent engagement becomes possible.
AI role: personalized pre-scan priorities based on last year's findings. "Last time, your LDL and waist circumference were the two things to move. Here's a focused 8-week plan." The intelligence layer creates a reason to rebook — and to care about the result.
