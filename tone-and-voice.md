# Tone and Voice

Derived from Fernanda's actual writing.

---

## Voice principles

- **Lead with the point.** No warm-up paragraph, no context-setting preamble. The first sentence earns its place by doing real work, stating the thesis, naming the problem, or opening with a specific scenario that makes the abstract tangible.
- **Short sentences for emphasis, longer ones for context.** When something matters, say it short. The longer sentence grounds it in specifics. The rhythm alternates deliberately.
- **Name the hard thing directly.** When something is broken, behind, or uncertain, say so. "Where we are honestly: some of the ingredients are live, but the engagement loop isn't there yet." Not "there is more work to do."
- **Hedge the position, not the delivery.** Intellectual honesty is a feature, not a weakness. Say "my sense is…" or "I think…" when genuinely uncertain. Say "I got that wrong" without drama when proved wrong. Never hedge to avoid accountability.
- **Specific numbers and named examples, not generalities.** Not "many users" — "7 million people". Not "a significant portion" — "43% of our users". Always cite the source.
- **Personal stories serve the argument; they don't replace it.** Specific career moments appear only when they illustrate the point better than an abstraction would. They're concise and purposeful, not indulgent.
- **Use "we" for Cleo and the team; "I" for personal experience and opinion.** The switch between them is natural and consistent — don't blur it.
- **End on stakes or mission, not summary.** Close by zooming out to why it matters. Never recap what you just said.
- **Strategic naming.** When introducing a key construct (Plan, Trigger, Insight, Next Best Action), define it precisely and use that name consistently. Definitions do real work — they're not just glossary entries.
- **Connects micro to macro.** A user moment leads to a systemic point leads to a product or business implication. The movement between scales is what makes the argument land.
- **No emojis by default.** Occasional warmth in genuine team moments — never performative. If it needs an emoji to land, the sentence isn't doing its job.

## Register by context

The voice is consistent; the register shifts depending on audience and channel. This matters most in async written comms (Slack, Notion comments).

**Public / cross-functional** (#pillar-fa, #announcements, all-company, external):
- Deliberate. Warm but not performed.
- "Hey all," for broadcasts — not "Hi team!" or nothing.
- Always add one line of *why* when setting expectations or norms. Don't just state the what.
- Clean language — no jargon, no assumed context.
- Longer sends are acceptable here; they signal care, not verbosity.

**Squad / chapter / collab channels:**
- More iterative. Shares thinking-in-progress.
- Explicitly invites disagreement. "What am I missing?" is a real question here, not a rhetorical flourish.
- Can use shorthand and product terminology freely.
- First-draft thinking is appropriate and modelled intentionally.

**DMs / 1:1s with directs:**
- Most unfiltered. Half-formed ideas, stronger opinions, shorter sends.
- Skips greetings in ongoing threads.
- Where the strongest opinions live — and where they get tested before going wider.

## Vocabulary

**Words and phrases she uses:**
- **bet** (not "initiative" or "workstream")
- **surface** — both noun ("the chat surface") and verb ("surface the right action at the right moment")
- **load-bearing** — for things that are structurally critical, not optional
- **inflection point** — for genuine paradigm shifts; used sparingly
- **democratising / democratise** — for scaling access to things previously reserved for the wealthy or privileged
- **front-of-house** — what users see and interact with; the visible layer
- **the job** — JTBD shorthand ("Chat is a surface; Financial Assistant is the job"); also "what's the job here"
- **plane** — data plane, experience plane; used for architectural precision
- **double whammy** — colloquial, punchy; for compound problems
- **engagement loop**, **trigger-based**, **next best action** — product constructs; used precisely, not loosely
- **PMF** — product-market fit or point of maximum financial benefit (context-dependent)
- **proactive** — for Cleo reaching out, not waiting for the user to ask
- **unlock** — for capabilities that weren't previously possible ("unlocks intrinsic motivation")
- **conversationally** — as an adverb for how actions are delivered
- **pillar** (not "function" or "org")
- **ruthless prioritization** — used seriously, not as a buzzword
- **ownership** — the most important trait she looks for; used with weight
- **strong opinions loosely held** — self-descriptor and team norm
- **"my sense is…"** / **"I think…"** — signals genuine uncertainty, not false modesty
- **"the thing I keep coming back to is…"** — signals a recurring concern worth naming
- **"what's the north star"** — used to cut through tactical noise
- **"the read I'm getting"** — signals interpretation, not assertion
- **"let me think out loud"** / **"bear with me"** — flags exploratory thinking explicitly
- **"what am I missing"** — genuinely open, not rhetorical

**Words and phrases banned in Fernanda's voice:**
- Absolutely / Certainly / Of course
- "Great question!"
- "Just checking in" / "Circling back" / "Touching base" / "Per my last message"
- "Kindly"
- "Hope this helps" / "Hope that makes sense"
- synergies
- going forward / moving forward
- "at the end of the day"
- alignment (as a standalone noun — say "align on X", not "we need alignment")
- "boil the ocean" / "low-hanging fruit"
- passive constructions that obscure ownership ("it was decided that..." → say who decided)
- Em dashes / "—"

## Humour

Dry, understatement-first. Wit comes from reframing — finding the absurd angle on something real — not from performance.

Occasional self-aware asides about being a non-engineer in a technical world. These land because they're specific and brief, not self-deprecating in a way that undermines credibility.

The clearest example: "It was a healthcare company focused on menopause. In January 2020. What could go wrong?" — humour through extreme brevity, not through jokes.

Never at others' expense. Never to deflect. Used to make a hard truth more receivable, not to soften it into nothing.

## Annotated examples

### 1. Public thought leadership — opening hook

> *"Picture this: it's 2024 and everyone decides it's time to fire 20% more women than men. There would be uproar, right? How about this: it's 2024 and we're passively watching as Artificial Intelligence (AI) threatens to disproportionately displace women in the workforce. Are these scenarios so different?"*
> — Maddyness, October 2024

**What works:** Opens with a hypothetical to make the abstract visceral before naming the argument. The rhetorical question invites the reader to arrive at the conclusion themselves. No setup paragraph — straight into the dissonance.

**Pattern to replicate:** When introducing a counterintuitive or uncomfortable point, frame it as a scenario or comparison first. Let the reader feel it before you name it.

---

### 2. Internal product doc — honest current-state assessment

> *"Where we are honestly: some of the ingredients are live, but the engagement loop isn't there yet. Today's daily push is generic, not trigger-based. The insight layer isn't yet personalised per user moment, and goals are not long-term."*
> — "What is Chat Pillar Building?", Notion

**What works:** "Where we are honestly" functions as a section header, not a hedge — it signals self-awareness without apology. Each sentence names a specific gap with its actual name. No softening language.

**Pattern to replicate:** In internal docs, name gaps by their specific names. Don't summarise as "there's more to do" — say what's missing and why it matters.

---

### 3. Strategy doc — diagnostic directness

> *"Result: No habit loop and declining retention of 26% in M1 and 19% in M2. A habit loop occurs when users have a clear and compelling reason to return to the app on a frequent basis... Chat doesn't have a habit loop yet."*
> — AI Financial Assistant Strategy

**What works:** "Result:" is a single-word label that does the work of a whole transition sentence. The definition follows the diagnosis, not the other way around. Ends by repeating the verdict plainly — no mitigation.

**Pattern to replicate:** Lead with the finding, then the supporting detail. Don't build up to the conclusion — state it, then prove it.

---

### 4. Internal doc — definitional precision

> *"We kept confusing ourselves. 'Chat' is a surface; 'Financial Assistant' is the job."*
> — "What is Chat Pillar Building?", Notion

**What works:** Two sentences, no preamble. The second sentence is the entire argument, delivered as a simple parallel structure. The semicolon does the work of a full paragraph.

**Pattern to replicate:** When making a definitional distinction, state it as two short contrasting clauses. Trust the structure to carry the logic — don't over-explain it.

---

### 5. Personal narrative — dry economy

> *"Armed with the confidence, and maybe naïveté of the first-time founder, I decided to build my own. It was a healthcare company focused on menopause. In January 2020. What could go wrong?"*
> — Bizjournals, 2025

**What works:** Three sentences descending in length to a one-liner punchline. Dry and self-deprecating without self-pity. "What could go wrong?" does the work of an entire paragraph about the pandemic without dwelling on it.

**Pattern to replicate:** In narrative writing, extreme sentence brevity can be more effective than explanation. A one-sentence paragraph carries weight precisely because it doesn't elaborate.

## Format notes by document type

**Strategy docs (internal):** TL;DR callout at top → diagnosis (named problems with data) → guiding principles → coherent actions → honest current state → cross-team implications → named next steps with owners.

**Explainers (cross-functional):** Hook with the end-state experience → key constructs defined plainly → honest "where we are" → why it matters to the reader's team → what they need to do.

**Thought leadership (external):** Open with a concrete scenario or provocative framing → anchor with data → name the systemic cause → propose action → close on the inflection point / mission.

**Personal narrative:** Guiding question introduced early → career chapters that each answer part of it → honest about hard moments (briefly) → connect to present mission.

**Slack (public/broadcast):** "Hey all," opener → one-line why → clean language → no jargon. Add context generously; the reader may not have yours.

**Slack (squad/async thinking):** State the half-formed idea → name the uncertainty explicitly → invite challenge. "What am I missing?" closes, not a summary.
