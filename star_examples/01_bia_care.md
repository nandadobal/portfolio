# STAR Example 1: Bia Care — Building a Regulated Virtual Clinic from Zero

**Role:** Co-founder & Product Lead  
**Theme:** 0→1 product, regulated environment, user obsession, commercial learning

---

## Situation

The menopause care market was massively underserved. HRT had a reputational problem, existing services were medication-only, and women were navigating a complex, fragmented system with no continuity of care. There was no tech-forward, whole-person approach that combined clinical oversight with lifestyle intervention — and no one had successfully brought this to the NHS digitally.

I co-founded Baya Care to fill that gap. We were a small product team: me, our CTO, and two engineers. My co-founder managed the clinical side. 

---

## Task

My task was to define the product strategy, own the build, and navigate CQC registration — all simultaneously. We needed to create a full-stack infrastructure for virtual group consultations: patient matching, EMR integration, video consultation rooms, clinical note-taking, and prescription workflows. And it had to work within NHS commissioning constraints as well as a private pay model.

---

## Action

We made a series of deliberate technical and clinical choices:

- **Tech stack:** Built the entire infrastructure for virtual group consultations — patient matching into cohorts, integration with Semble (our EMR), a video consultation layer for group sessions, and a post-consultation flow that auto-uploaded clinical notes back into the EMR.
- **Prescription pipeline:** Integrated prescriptions end-to-end so clinicians could complete the full workflow inside one system, reducing admin burden and error.
- **CQC registration:** Navigated Care Quality Commission requirements to become a registered clinic — essential for NHS commissioning and trust-building.
- **Research-led design:** I visited menopause cafes and attended community events to understand what women actually needed. One key insight: doctor + medication were the trust unlock. Even when women wanted a holistic lifestyle programme, they wouldn't commit without that anchor.
- **Failure and pivot:** Our first product was a 12-week lifestyle programme. It piloted well, NPS was high, the waiting list looked strong. But when the time came to pay, conversion was low. What people said they wanted and what they were willing to pay for were different things. We killed it and refocused on the clinical core, which became the trust bridge to everything else.
- **Most successful use case:** Women going through breast cancer — a population with acute need, high pain point (10/10 on our scale), and significant clinical complexity that our group model handled particularly well. We also found that people wanted clinical treatment first, and then they would come back for repeated support. 

---

## Result

- **NPS of 86** across the patient population
- Became the **first private provider commissioned by the NHS** to deliver patient-facing menopause services, covering a patient population of 100,000+
- Measured subscription-to-prescription uptake and repeat session rates as the core product health signals
- Won the **Royal College of GPs annual conference research award** for service innovation (2021)
- The failure of the first product made me significantly more commercial: it taught me to test willingness to pay early, to separate what users say they want from what they'll act on, and to understand the P&L of the whole business — not just the product metrics

---

## Key Talking Points for Interviews

- This was genuine 0→1 in a regulated environment — not just a new feature, but a registered clinic with clinical governance
- The pivot story shows commercial maturity: I didn't cling to the first product because the NPS was good; I followed the money signal
- The use of JTBD thinking (demand-side sales) informed how I structured the product around anxieties and unlocks, not stated preferences
- Relevant for health-tech roles: I understand the intersection of regulation, clinical workflow, and product experience
