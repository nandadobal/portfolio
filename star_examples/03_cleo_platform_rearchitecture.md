# STAR Example 3: Cleo — Re-architecting the AI Platform (Player-Coach Model)

**Role:** Principal Product Director  
**Theme:** Senior leadership, player-coaching, platform bets, multi-agent AI architecture

---

## Situation

When I joined Cleo, the product was an amalgamation of different approaches built up over time — siloed teams, disempowered design, a fragmented tech stack that no single person fully understood. This created imperfect user experiences: the AI could only talk about a narrow set of topics, the architecture was brittle, and there was no shared vision for what Cleo's conversational layer should actually be.

It was clear that without a fundamental architectural change, Cleo's AI would remain a collection of features rather than a coherent platform. And the window to act was closing — the industry was moving fast.

---

## Task

My task was to bring Cleo's architecture into the future — moving to a tools-based, multi-agent approach that would let Cleo's AI talk about anything, not just the topics it had been explicitly programmed for. This was a strategic platform bet, not an incremental product improvement.

I operated in a player-coach role: I directly led a shadow squad (a small, fast team operating adjacent to the main org) while also setting direction for a larger pillar of ~50 people.

---

## Action

**Phase 1: Proof of concept (shadow squad, ~a few weeks)**
I PMed a small shadow squad to build a prototype of the new architecture. We worked fast, outside the main roadmap, to prove the concept was viable before asking the organisation to commit to it. This is the standard trap with platform work: POCs are easy, especially with Gen AI. Getting from 50% to 80% and then 80% to 100% is where the real work is — and I was clear about that from the start.

The prototype was enough to get buy-in from our CPO. I brought them on the journey early, showing the vision and the path rather than presenting a fait accompli.

**Phase 2: Build (~2 months)**
With leadership alignment secured, we moved into a focused build phase. The architecture shift was to a tools-based, multi-agent model — essentially creating harnesses that let Cleo's AI call different capabilities depending on what the user needed. This is the infrastructure everyone in the industry is talking about now; we were building it a year ahead of the curve.

**Phase 3: Rollout (~2 months, ~50 people)**
Coordinated the rollout across a pillar of ~50 people. The four metrics we tracked relentlessly:

1. **Eval quality** — the most important. If the AI's outputs weren't better, nothing else mattered. This was our internal quality gate.
2. **CS contact rate** — a proxy for confusion, failure states, and trust erosion
3. **Conversion** — into paid products; the business case
4. **M1 retention** — did users come back? Did the new architecture make Cleo stickier?

---

## Result

- Successfully rolled out to **100% of users**
- All four metrics moved in the right direction
- The tools-based, multi-agent architecture now positions Cleo to have conversations about *anything* — a fundamental expansion of the product's surface area
- The architectural decision has strategic compounding value: every new capability can now be added as a tool, rather than requiring a rebuild
- From a competitive standpoint: I shudder to think what would have happened if we hadn't done this. The industry caught up fast.

---

## Key Talking Points for Interviews

- This is a senior leadership example: I both did the work (shadow squad PM) and led the organisation (pillar of 50)
- Platform bets require a different kind of buy-in — I didn't ask for permission upfront, I built a credible POC first and then brought leadership on the journey
- The four metrics were deliberately chosen to balance quality (eval), trust (CS contact rate), growth (conversion), and retention (M1) — not just output metrics
- The multi-agent/tools architecture is directly relevant to anyone building AI products today — this is the architecture everyone is converging on
- The player-coach dynamic (doing and leading simultaneously) is a good signal for principal-level roles
